The course

Seven things. Then one ad.

Theory first, then the tool. The morning covers what retail runs on — straight and simple, with one Singapore example each. The afternoon turns it into a social ad, because all seven point the same way.

Morning What retail runs on

01

What retailing is

Selling directly to the final consumer — and the six things every retailer settles: product, price, place, personnel, promotion and presentation.

02

Target markets

Segmenting shoppers by who they are, where they live, how they think and how ready they are to buy — and the tests a segment must pass to be worth targeting.

03

Service levels

Where the retailer stands between producer, wholesaler and customer, and the service ladder from self-service to AI-assisted.

04

Retail formats

Store-based, non-store and hybrid — and the three forces driving growth: consumer trends, demographics and technology.

05

Omni-channel

Why physical and digital channels must run as one — same stock, same price, one view of the shopper — or the sale is lost at the break.

06

Key issues

What presses from outside — e-commerce, manpower, rent, sustainability, AI risk — and what a manager controls inside: availability, atmosphere, service.

07

Technology in retail

The tech that runs the business, the tech that changes the shopper's experience — AR, VR, AI chatbots — and what it takes to run both.

Shopping starts on the feed

Read the seven together and they say one thing: the retailer has to be on the feed. That's why the afternoon is a social ad.

Afternoon One ad, four acts

One Google Flow generation per act, eight seconds each — a 32-second ad with your character, product and scene locked by reference sheets and joined by invisible cuts. An AI chat directs the plan with you; Suno scores it; CapCut finishes it.

0–8 s

Act 1Hook

Stop the scroll in two seconds. Show the need.

8–16 s

Act 2Solution

16–24 s

Act 3Gratification

24–32 s

Act 4Call to action

The character tells the shopper what to do now.

The toolkit

Three tools. One workflow.

Plus any AI chat — ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini — as your ad director. It firms up your product, price and shopper, writes three versions of the four acts, and hands you a locked pack to paste into Flow.

Step one

Google Flow

Direct

Google's AI video studio. Build reference sheets that lock your character, product and scene, then generate your four acts, eight seconds each. No camera, no crew, no location.

Step two

Suno

Score

Generate an original instrumental track, about 40 seconds, that fits your ad's mood. Yours to use, labelled as AI-made, free of licensing headaches.

Step three

CapCut

Cut & publish

Join the four acts into one, add captions and the end card, and export at the format your platform needs — vertical or landscape, ready to post.

Questions

Before you book.

Is it all AI video, or is there retail theory too?
Both, in that order. The morning covers seven retail fundamentals — what retailing is, target markets, service levels, formats, omni-channel, key issues and technology — with one Singapore example each. The afternoon applies them: one social ad whose job and call to action follow from your shopper and where you sell.
Do I need video or design experience?
No. The course is built for beginners and runs live, step by step. If you can write a sentence and use a web browser, you can direct a shot in Google Flow. Learners with no prior experience have produced their own video by the end of a session.
Can I use my SkillsFuture Credit?
Eligible Singaporeans can use SkillsFuture Credit to offset the fee for this SkillsFuture-registered course (ref. TGS-2022016568). PSEA and Absentee Payroll for sponsoring SMEs may also apply. Eligibility rules and the current nett fees are set out on the Tembusu Institute course page and MySkillsFuture.
What will I actually have at the end of the day?
A four-act, 32-second social ad you directed yourself, with a call to action, an original Suno track and captions, exported at the format your platform needs — plus a one-page brief and a still frame. Ready to post. And the Tembusu Certificate once you pass the assessment.
What if I don't have a product to advertise?
Bring your own if you have one. If not, the ad-director prompt carries a menu of ready-made products, characters and settings — pick numbers and start directing.
Is there an assessment?
Yes, at the end of the day. A 30-minute practical — your finished ad plus a one-page brief showing your product, price, place and target shopper — then a 30-minute written paper of seven short questions, one per morning topic. You rehearse every question in class; it is designed to confirm what you've learned, not to trip you up.
What do I need to bring?
A laptop with a charger, a Google account, and headphones if you like to work with sound. Google Flow requires a Google AI Pro subscription; CapCut has a free tier and Suno has a free plan for practice. Tembusu will confirm the exact account requirements when you register.
Where is the class held?
On campus at Tembusu Institute — Central Plaza, 298 Tiong Bahru Road, or Thye Hong Centre, 2 Leng Kee Road, Singapore. Your venue is confirmed on registration.
Who teaches it?
Dr. Andrew Chew of Impactus Learning designs and delivers the programme with Tembusu Institute. He is an award-winning trainer, lectures in AI and Marketing at SUSS and NTU, and has taught more than 1,200 professionals in generative AI.
